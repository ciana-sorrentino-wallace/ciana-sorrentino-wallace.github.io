Case study · IU Public Policy Institute (CRISP) · 2021

What makes a peer-helper program work, and what was wearing its volunteers down

The Immigrant Welcome Center in Indianapolis asked an evaluation clinic what was working in its Natural Helpers program and where it could improve. I built the program’s logic model and its first real measure of training impact, and helped turn volunteers’ experiences into practical changes.

01

The challenge

Natural Helpers are immigrants who help newer immigrants and refugees navigate life in Indianapolis. The program had grown for 15 years on trust and goodwill, but had little structured evidence of what it was achieving.

COVID-19 sharply increased what volunteers were being asked to do, from rent and utilities to health care and mental health. The Immigrant Welcome Center wanted to know what made the program effective, how comparable programs keep volunteers engaged, and whether its trainings and requirements were helping or getting in the way.

02

My approach

  1. Built the theory of changeWorked with program staff to map how the program’s resources and activities are supposed to lead to outcomes for volunteers, newcomers and the organization.
  2. Reviewed the evidenceScreened more than 20 studies of comparable programs, including Natural Helpers in Dayton, Ohio, Hartford’s Cultural Navigator Program and the Promotores de Salud model.
  3. Listened to the volunteersInterviewed Natural Helpers and program leadership, and analyzed a focus group with nine Natural Helpers and Specialists.
  4. Reviewed the paperworkContracts, code of ethics, training manuals, help forms and existing training evaluations.
  5. Designed a better measureCreated a pretest/posttest instrument so the organization could show what each training changed, not just whether people liked it.
03

The logic model

The working theory of change gave the organization a shared picture of how its everyday work connects to its mission, and what it should measure along the way.

Inputs

  • Funding for stipends and Specialist roles
  • Partner organizations that train
  • WhatsApp, Zoom and Facebook channels
  • About 30 new volunteers a year

Activities

  • Monthly trainings and orientations
  • Weekly calls and crisis trainings
  • Helping newcomers as needs arise
  • Help-form tracking and follow-up

Outputs

  • Trainings held and attendance
  • Pretest and posttest results
  • Help forms submitted
  • Volunteer hours logged

Outcomes

  • An engaged, representative volunteer network
  • Volunteers who are more confident advocates
  • Newcomers who know where to find trusted help

Impact

  • Immigrants and refugees gain access to power in the city
  • Newcomers find resources quickly
  • Stronger advocacy for immigrant needs in Indiana
04

What we found

#1Strength

The volunteers themselves

Unlike comparable programs that rely on volunteers who have never immigrated, Natural Helpers come from the communities they serve and were often helping neighbors long before joining. That builds trust faster than training can.

8Required trainings a year

Valued, but too many and too unclear

Volunteers loved the legal, naturalization and self-care sessions, but trainings, webinars and partner calls ran together, and it was not clear which were required. Most suggested four to six a year.

2 minIdeal help-form time

Data collection was the biggest burden

Volunteers wanted a help form that takes a couple of minutes. They became more willing once they understood the data supports program funding.

0Baseline measures

Training evaluations could not show change

Existing post-session surveys captured satisfaction, but without a baseline they could not show whether a training changed what people knew.

“What’s really special about the Natural Helper program is that everyone I know who has come through it is truly a natural helper.”

Natural Helper, focus group participant
05

What we recommended

A training menuDifferent levels for different skills, clearly labeled required and optional sessions, a manageable number per year and an annual refresher.
Flexible participationLet volunteers pause and return, drop the “alumni” label, and replace annual renewal with a light check-in every six months.
Help forms as status updatesShort updates that also collect feedback, training requests and what is going well.
Matched measurementUse the new pretest/posttest instrument for every training, aligned to the logic model, so the organization can report change over time.
